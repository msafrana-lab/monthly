Alternative investments in September

Equities and bonds fell together in September. Our diversifying strategies posted gains.

2-minute read · Data as at 30 September 2026

Amid this turbulence, alternative strategies did their job. In September, equities and bonds declined together, a configuration in which the traditional balance between the two offers a portfolio little protection. Our , the investments mentioned in the letter that do not depend on which way markets move, gained over the month. They are run by managers whose performance owes little to the direction of equities and bonds. They benefit from the wide performance dispersion between companies, sectors, currencies and rates, which has increased in recent months. That is why we are allocating a growing share of our portfolios to them.

In practice, these managers do not seek to profit from a general rise in markets. They buy what they consider cheap and sell what they consider expensive, or take positions on the gap between two currencies or two interest rates. Their results therefore depend mainly on the accuracy of these choices, and little on the direction of the indices, which makes them a useful complement when equities and bonds fall at the same time.

Why equities and bonds can fall togetherWhen inflation worries investors more than growth

In a traditional portfolio, bonds generally cushion falls in equities: when the economy slows, rates decline and bond prices rise.

This balance works poorly when inflation, rather than growth, is the main concern. Central banks then raise rates, which pushes bond prices down and weighs on equities at the same time. That was the configuration in September, and it is why we complement equities and bonds with other sources of return.
