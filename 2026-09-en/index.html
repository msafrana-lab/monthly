1Interest rates

Rates are set to stay higher for longer

5.29%The US 10-year yield at end-September, its highest level since 2007.

The Fed raised rates for the first time since 2023, to 4.00%, following the ECB (2.50%), and the Bank of Japan followed suit. Fed policymakers no longer project any cut before the end of 2027, and the US 10-year yield climbed from 4.75% to 5.29%.

Looking aheadThe Fed and ECB meetings on 28 and 29 October will show whether the tightening continues. We are locking in these higher yields through short- to intermediate-dated maturities.
Read the analysis
2Switzerland

The rate gap weighs on the franc and on Swiss equities

0%The SNB policy rate, held on 24 September.

The SNB held at 0% while its peers tightened. Drawn to higher returns elsewhere, capital moved out of the franc, which fell to a sixteen-month low against the dollar, and Swiss dividend stocks lost some of their appeal.

Looking aheadAs a currency, the franc can rebound quickly. After this pullback, the Swiss market trades at attractive valuations, and its industrial companies are well placed to benefit from the European recovery.
Read the analysis
3Energy

Energy remains expensive, but Europe has enough to get through the winter

+27%The rise in physical Brent in September, to USD 114 a barrel on the 29th.

An attack on a Saudi pipeline on 10 September pushed above USD 130 a barrel. Gas is the greater concern: EU storage, at around 71% of capacity, is at its lowest level for the time of year since 2011.

Looking aheadEurope now consumes around 18% less gas than in 2021, and Copernicus forecasts a milder-than-average winter. A late cold snap cannot be ruled out, however.

Concept of the month
Interest rate differential

The difference in yield between two currencies or two borrowers.

Invested short term, a franc earns almost nothing, while a dollar earns around 4%. The wider this gap, the greater the incentive for capital to move from the franc into the dollar, as it did in September.
